Do you need a permit to remove a tree in Florida?

Last updated November 21, 2026 · Written for Florida homeowners by Florida Tree Service Match

Short answer

Short answer: Sometimes. Florida law (s. 163.045) bars local governments from requiring a permit, fee or mitigation to prune, trim or remove a tree on a single-family residential property if you have signed onsite documentation from an ISA-certified arborist or Florida-licensed landscape architect showing the tree is an unacceptable risk. Otherwise, your city or county's tree ordinance applies.

  1. The state exemption
  2. When local rules apply

The state exemption

Section 163.045 applies to single-family detached homes actively used as such. The documentation must show the tree poses an unacceptable risk to persons or property, meaning removal is the only practical way to reduce risk below moderate.[1]

When local rules apply

Without that documentation, local tree-protection rules can apply, especially to larger or protected species. Check with your city or county before removal.[1]

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a permit to remove a dangerous tree at my Florida home?

Not if you have signed onsite documentation from an ISA-certified arborist or Florida-licensed landscape architect that it poses an unacceptable risk, and the property is a single-family home.[1]
