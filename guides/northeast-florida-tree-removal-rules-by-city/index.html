Tree removal rules in Northeast Florida, city by city (2026)

Last updated November 21, 2026 · Written for Florida homeowners by Florida Tree Service Match

Short answer

Short answer: Most Northeast Florida cities require a permit to remove larger trees, with thresholds from 3 inches DBH (St. Augustine) to 8 inches (Neptune Beach). Unincorporated St. Johns County generally does not require one on lots under an acre with an existing home, except for specimen trees, conservation areas and the Ponte Vedra Zoning District. Everywhere, s. 163.045 lets a single-family homeowner with signed arborist documentation of unacceptable risk skip local permits, fees and replanting.

The state rule (s. 163.045)

Documentation means an onsite assessment under the ISA Tree Risk Assessment Best Management Practices (2nd ed., 2017), signed by an ISA-certified arborist or Florida-licensed landscape architect. It applies to single-family detached homes. Local governments also may not require replanting of a tree removed under this section. It does not apply to delegated mangrove protection.[1]

St. Johns County cities

St. Augustine: permit for any tree 3 inches DBH or larger and for Southern Red Cedars over 2 feet tall; the city lists a $30 permit from the Planning and Building Department.

St. Augustine Beach: protected trees need a removal permit, and trees 30 inches DBH or larger need Comprehensive Planning and Zoning Board approval.

Unincorporated St. Johns County: no permit on lots under one acre with an existing home unless the tree is a specimen tree or in a conservation area or buffer; lots in the Ponte Vedra Zoning District need a Protected Tree Removal Permit.[2][3][4][5][6]

Duval beaches

Jacksonville Beach: trees 6 inches DBH or more are protected unless listed as unprotected; the city's FAQ says no permit is needed when you comply with s. 163.045.

Atlantic Beach: permits are required for regulated trees under Chapter 23, with exemptions for storm emergencies and imminent safety hazards.

Neptune Beach: its application says a permit is required for trees 8 inches DBH and greater.[7][8][9]

Clay and Nassau

Town of Orange Park: unreplaced protected trees carry mitigation of $148 per inch DBH ($165 for historic trees).

Unincorporated Clay County: the county's tree-protection article exempts single-family lots of record before January 28, 2003 and lots not in a major subdivision created after that date; confirm with Clay County Zoning.

Fernandina Beach: protected trees are healthy non-invasive trees 5 inches DBH or larger; trees an ISA-certified arborist or licensed landscape architect assesses as hazardous, dead, dying or diseased need no removal permit.[10][11][12]

Frequently asked questions

Does a dead tree need a permit in Fernandina Beach?

The city's FAQ says yes if it is 5 inches DBH or larger, but its Tree Permitting page says trees an ISA-certified arborist or licensed landscape architect assesses as dead, dying, diseased or hazardous need no removal permit. Get the assessment in writing.[13][12]
