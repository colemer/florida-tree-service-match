What to look for in a tree service company

Four checks before you sign anything. How our matching works.

Check 1 · Credentials

Ask about arborist credentials

  • Ask whether an ISA-certified arborist will assess the tree
  • At a single-family home, signed risk documentation from an ISA-certified arborist can affect whether a local permit is needed
  • Check any license the company claims on DBPR's free Verify a License search

Source[2][3]

Check 2 · Insurance

Proof of insurance

  • Ask for a current certificate of general liability insurance in the company's name
  • Ask whether the crew working at your home is covered by workers' compensation
  • Call the insurer on the certificate to confirm the policy is active

General guidance

Check 3 · Estimate

A written, itemized estimate

  • Scope, materials and quantities in writing, not just a total
  • Who applies for any permit, and who pays the permit fee
  • Start date, expected timeline, cleanup and haul-away

General guidance

Check 4 · Payment

Fair payment terms and a warranty

  • Be wary of paying most of the price before work starts
  • Tie payments to finished stages of the job
  • Get the workmanship and product warranties in writing

Frequently asked questions

Is this a tree company?

No. Florida Tree Service Match is a free referral service. With your consent, we can pass your request to up to 3 independent local tree companies named on our form.

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Northeast Florida?

Often, depending on the city and the trunk size: 3 inches DBH in St. Augustine, 5 in Fernandina Beach, 6 in Jacksonville Beach. Florida's s. 163.045 removes the permit requirement at a single-family home if you have signed arborist documentation that the tree is an unacceptable risk.[4][5][6][2]

What does tree removal cost?

Angi's 2026 national average is about $750, typically $200 to $2,000.[1]
