Storm history: trees and wind

Recent storms that brought down trees or flooded this area, from NOAA, NWS and local reports.

Flood risk and FEMA maps

The city sits on a barrier island, exposed to ocean surge and overwash on the east and Intracoastal flooding on the west. Check an address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center; oceanfront lots can be in coastal high-hazard zones with stricter rebuild rules.[2][7]

Who issues tree permits in Jacksonville Beach?

City of Jacksonville Beach Building Inspection Division. Phone: 904-247-6235. Jacksonville Beach issues its own permits (online E-Permit or in person at 11 N. 3rd St.). It is not part of the consolidated City of Jacksonville.[6]

Tree rules differ by city and county. Florida's s. 163.045 overrides local permits for a risky tree at a single-family home if you hold signed documentation from an ISA-certified arborist or Florida-licensed landscape architect; otherwise ask the office before cutting.

Jacksonville Beach tree service FAQs

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Jacksonville Beach?

Jacksonville Beach protects any tree 6 inches or more in diameter unless it is specifically listed as unprotected, and a fine can be assessed for removing a protected tree without a permit.[4]

What else applies to tree removal in Jacksonville Beach?

The city's FAQ and residential permit checklist both say no permit, fee or mitigation is needed for a residential tree when the owner complies with s. 163.045 (arborist documentation).[4][5]

How do I apply to remove a protected tree in Jacksonville Beach?

Submit the city's Tree Removal Application to Planning & Development; if the tree meets the removal criteria, the city issues a permit.[4]
