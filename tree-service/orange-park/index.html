Tree service in Orange Park: what's different here

Orange Park's town rules put a dollar figure on unreplaced protected trees, while nearby county addresses follow Clay County's rules. Irma's 11-plus inches of rain and Black Creek flooding are the recent storm benchmark.

  • The Town of Orange Park sets mitigation for protected trees removed and not replaced: $148 per inch DBH, or $165 per inch for historic protected trees.[4]
  • Many Orange Park mailing addresses are outside town limits and fall under Clay County rules instead, including the county's exemption for older single-family lots.[5][6]

Storm history: trees and wind

Recent storms that brought down trees or flooded this area, from NOAA, NWS and local reports.

  • Hurricane Irma (September 11, 2017): 11.03 inches of rain fell 1.8 miles west-southwest of Orange Park, and the St. Johns River at the Buckman Bridge set a record 5.63-foot flood stage.[1][2]
  • Hurricane Irma (Black Creek) (September 12, 2017): Black Creek at Middleburg crested at a record 30.52 feet, and helicopter rescues were needed along the creek.[1][3]

Flood risk and FEMA maps

Riverfront streets in the town face St. Johns River flooding, while creek-side neighborhoods in the wider Orange Park area face Black Creek and tributary flooding. Check each address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center.[1][8]

Who issues tree permits in Orange Park?

Town of Orange Park Economic & Community Development (in town); Clay County Building Division (outside town). Phone: Town 904-264-2635; Clay County 904-284-6307. Many 'Orange Park' mailing addresses, including much of Oakleaf (32065), are outside town limits and are permitted by Clay County, not the town.[5][7]

Tree rules differ by city and county. Florida's s. 163.045 overrides local permits for a risky tree at a single-family home if you hold signed documentation from an ISA-certified arborist or Florida-licensed landscape architect; otherwise ask the office before cutting.
