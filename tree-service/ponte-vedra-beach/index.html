Storm history: trees and wind

Recent storms that brought down trees or flooded this area, from NOAA, NWS and local reports.

  • Hurricane Irma (September 11, 2017): Homes along Roscoe Boulevard, which backs onto the Intracoastal Waterway, woke to surge flooding, downed trees and power lines.[1]
  • Hurricane Ian (September 29, 2022): FDEP rated a roughly 3.5-mile stretch of Ponte Vedra shoreline as moderate beach and dune erosion (Condition III).[2]
  • Hurricane Nicole (November 10, 2022): State Road A1A in South Ponte Vedra Beach washed out; FDEP measured about 465,000 cubic yards of sand lost along Ponte Vedra across Ian and Nicole, with moderate to major erosion along the developed shoreline.[3][4][5]

Flood risk and FEMA maps

Two different flood risks: oceanfront homes face wave overwash and erosion, while homes on the Intracoastal side (Roscoe Boulevard, Palm Valley) flood from surge pushed up the waterway. Check each address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center.[1][4][9]

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Ponte Vedra Beach?

What else applies to tree removal in Ponte Vedra Beach?

Does the arborist exemption override Ponte Vedra's tree permit?

For a risky tree at a single-family detached home, yes: s. 163.045 bars local governments from requiring a permit, fee or mitigation when you hold signed documentation from an ISA-certified arborist or Florida-licensed landscape architect.[11]

How long does a Ponte Vedra protected tree permit last?

The ordinance says a Protected Tree Removal Permit expires six months after it is issued.[6]
