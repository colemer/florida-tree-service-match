Tree service in Winter Park: what's different here

Winter Park's urban forestry rules attach a dollar figure to replacements: 3-inch minimum caliper, 30 days to plant, and a bill plus $150 if you don't.

  • A residential tree removal permit in Winter Park is $35; replacement trees must be Florida #1 grade with at least a 3-inch caliper, planted within 30 days or the city bills the inches owed plus $150.[3]
  • For trees near primary power lines, Winter Park clears a 10-foot radius at $150 per hour, or the owner can hire a qualified line-clearance contractor.[3]

Who issues tree permits in Winter Park?

City of Winter Park Building & Permitting Services (Permitting Self-Service Portal); Engineering for right-of-way. Phone: Inspection line 407-599-3350; Engineering 407-450-0815; Public Works 407-599-3233. Right-of-way utilization permits, tree removal permits and building permits are all filed through the city's Permitting Self-Service Portal.[4][3]

Tree rules differ by city and county. Florida's s. 163.045 overrides local permits for a risky tree at a single-family home if you hold signed documentation from an ISA-certified arborist or Florida-licensed landscape architect; otherwise ask the office before cutting.

Winter Park tree service FAQs

What does a Winter Park tree removal permit cost?

$35 for residential, $100 for a Tree Preservation Board appeal and $25 per re-inspection. Apply through the Permitting Self-Service Portal; staff inspect in the field.[3]

What are the replacement rules?

Replacements must be Florida #1 and at least 3 inches in caliper. Extra caliper inches planted cut the tree-trust-fund deposit by $110 each, and missing the 30-day planting window brings a bill for the inches owed plus a $150 fee.[3]

What if the tree is near power lines?

For primary wires, the city clears a 10-foot radius at $150 per hour, or you can hire a qualified line-clearance contractor. Secondary and service wires go through customer service at 877-811-8700.[3]

What about a dangerous tree?

Under s. 163.045, a qualifying single-family owner with certified-arborist documentation that the tree is a danger can remove it without a permit.[7]

What to look for in a Winter Park tree service company

Four checks before you sign anything. How our matching works.

Check 1 · Credentials

Ask about arborist credentials

  • Ask whether an ISA-certified arborist will assess the tree
  • At a single-family home, signed risk documentation from an ISA-certified arborist can affect whether a local permit is needed
  • Check any license the company claims on DBPR's free Verify a License search

Check 2 · Insurance

Proof of insurance

  • Ask for a current certificate of general liability insurance in the company's name
  • Ask whether the crew working at your home is covered by workers' compensation
  • Call the insurer on the certificate to confirm the policy is active
